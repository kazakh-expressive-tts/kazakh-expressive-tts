Abstract

Current expressive speech synthesis models are constrained by the limited availability of open-source datasets containing diverse non-verbal vocalizations (NVs). This paper introduces KazExpTTS, the first open-source expressive speech synthesis dataset for Kazakh, a low-resource language spoken by over 13 million people worldwide. The dataset contains 2.83 hours of speech from two speakers and covers six emotion categories and nine types of NVs, including laughter, sighs, and coughs. To demonstrate the effectiveness of KazExpTTS, we fine-tune a state-of-the-art Orpheus-TTS model and evaluate it using both objective and subjective metrics. Experimental results show that the model generates natural and expressive speech with NVs while maintaining intelligibility and speaker consistency. Notably, the model generalizes effectively to speakers without NV annotations in the training data, enabling NV generation for such speakers. The dataset, source code, and pretrained models are publicly released to support research in this area.
